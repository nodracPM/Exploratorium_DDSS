#### About

This webpage presents an interactive exploration of the research landscape at the Department of Mathematical Modeling of Social Sciences. Using tools from Formal Concept Analysis, we visualize how members of the department relate to one another through their lines of research, methodological approaches, and objects of study. Rather than offering a simple list or taxonomy, the visualizations reveal the conceptual structure of the department as a whole.

#### Concept lattices as maps of research

The core of this site consists of concept lattices that organize academic staff according to shared attributes. Each lattice represents a formal context in which researchers are related to specific themes, methods, or empirical domains. The resulting structures make explicit both commonalities and distinctions, showing how individual profiles intersect, overlap, or remain specialized within the broader research ecosystem.

#### Lines of research

The Lines of Research section highlights the main theoretical and thematic orientations developed within the department. The lattice representation allows users to see how these lines are not isolated silos, but interconnected trajectories that may converge around shared problems, concepts, or modeling strategies. This view emphasizes the department’s intellectual diversity while also revealing points of conceptual coherence.

#### Methodological approaches

In the Methodologies section, researchers are organized according to the analytical, mathematical, computational, and qualitative tools they employ. The lattice structure makes visible methodological affinities that cut across research areas, illustrating how techniques such as cientometrics, interviews, simulation, network analysis, or computational methods function as bridges between otherwise distinct domains of inquiry.

#### Objects of study

The Objects of Study section focuses on the empirical and conceptual phenomena addressed by the department, ranging from social systems and institutions to cultural, economic, and technological processes. By visualizing these objects in a lattice, the page highlights how different researchers may engage with the same object from multiple perspectives, or how a single research profile may span several empirical domains.

#### Why lattices?

These visualizations are not merely descriptive but epistemic tools. Concept lattices allow us to represent research profiles as structured combinations of attributes, making explicit the implicit organization of academic work. They support exploration, comparison, and reflection, offering a dynamic map of the department’s research activity that can evolve as interests, methods, and collaborations change over time.

- Compare contexts with shared domains.

The methodological approach of the *Exploratorium* is grounded in the examination and construction of hierarchical networks known as lattices. This class of networks is distinguished by its properties as partially ordered sets, establishing a comprehensive hierarchy.

#### Hierarchical networks

Lattices have the property that for any pair of nodes there is only one minimal node and one supreme node. 

#### Formal Concept Analysis

Starting from formal contexts represented by incidence tables (G,M,i), we use the methodology of **Formal Concept Analysis (FCA)**, a mathematical theory based on Birkhoff lattice theory and the Galois connection, to visualize and explore concept lattices. FCA systematically recovers formal concepts (certain ordered pairs of objects and attributes), organizes them hierarchically and represents them as frameworks, offering a deep insight into the structural interdependencies of the concepts.
